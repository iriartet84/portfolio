excerpt: >
  This paper examines whether institutional quality conditions fiscal responses
  to exogenous oil supply shocks in oil-importing economies. Using
  state-dependent local projections with a composite institutional index on 29
  countries (1974–2024), I find that primary balance responses diverge sharply
  across institutional regimes. A fiscal decomposition reveals that the
  asymmetry operates through revenue and debt channels rather than expenditure
  adjustment. A smooth-transition extension shows that property rights, rule of
  law, and bureaucratic quality act as near-discrete thresholds for
  international capital market access, underscoring the centrality of state
  capacity for fiscal space. Institutional credibility thus emerges as the
  primary mechanism through which institutional quality shapes fiscal resilience
  to energy shocks.
